# Terrace House: Boys & Girls in the City — Place Inventory

Scope: all 46 episodes of the first Netflix-produced Tokyo season (2015–2016), the season with 17 residents. A “place” is counted at the smallest useful scene level: each room/zone in the house is separate; a specific shop inside a larger complex is separate, while the mall itself is not counted unless the cast used a shared mall space as the scene.

## 1. The Terrace House itself — distinct places and layout

The house was in the Gotanda/Shinagawa area of Tokyo. No reliable public architectural floor plan was found, so the layout below is a scene-derived functional layout, not a construction drawing.

| Place ID | Room or zone | Layout / visual relationship |
|---|---|---|
| H01 | Entrance / genkan | Street-side entry; opens into the internal circulation area. Used for arrivals, departures, greetings, and luggage. |
| H02 | Main living room | Large sunken/lounge-style communal seating zone with sofas and TV; visually connected to the dining/kitchen side and facing the glass doors toward the terrace. |
| H03 | Dining area | Long communal dining table between/alongside the living room and kitchen; many house meetings and meals happen here. |
| H04 | Kitchen | Open-plan kitchen with island/counter, sink, cooktop, storage, and refrigerator; directly connected to dining area. |
| H05 | Playroom / TV room | Separate, more private lounge used for one-on-one conversations and group TV viewing; distinct from the main living room. |
| H06 | Boys’ bedroom | Shared male sleeping room with three beds and personal storage. Hayato’s bed is specifically important late in the season. |
| H07 | Girls’ bedroom | Shared female sleeping room with three beds, vanity/storage, and its own conversation zone. |
| H08 | Japanese-style room (washitsu) | Upstairs tatami room discovered/used in Episode 17; proposed as a sleeping space for Minori and Uchi. |
| H09 | Main bathroom / bathing room | Enclosed wet room/bath used by residents; count separately from sink/toilet areas. |
| H10 | Washroom / vanity area | Sink, mirrors, grooming/laundry transition area adjacent to bathing functions. |
| H11 | Toilet | Separate toilet room, consistent with the Japanese bath layout. |
| H12 | Laundry / utility area | Washer/household utility zone; functionally distinct even when adjacent to washroom. |
| H13 | Hallway and staircase | Internal circulation joining common rooms, bedrooms, and upstairs washitsu. |
| H14 | Terrace / pool deck | Outdoor paved social zone immediately outside the main glazing; used for conversations, parties, and lounging. |
| H15 | Swimming pool | Separate water zone; used repeatedly, including Episode 2 and the finale. |
| H16 | Barbecue area | Outdoor grill/dining portion of the terrace; used for the Episode 31–32 barbecue and finale. |
| H17 | Front exterior / driveway | Arrival/departure and vehicle-loading area outside the entrance. |
| H18 | Garage / car area | House vehicle storage/parking zone; count only when the scene actually occurs there. |

### Functional layout (approximate)

- Public core: entrance → living room → dining area → open kitchen.
- Outdoor core: living/dining glazing → terrace/pool deck → pool; barbecue equipment sits on the terrace side.
- Private wing/floor: boys’ bedroom, girls’ bedroom, bathing/washroom/toilet areas.
- Upper/secondary space: Japanese-style tatami room, reached by the interior stairs.
- The playroom is enclosed enough to function as a private conversation room, unlike the open living/dining/kitchen core.

## 4. Practical “world map” structure

For a game or structured dataset, use the IDs above as atomic places. Parent areas such as “Yokohama,” “Shibuya,” “Odaiba,” “Nagatoro,” or “the house” should be containers, not visitable places, unless a scene takes place in a public common area. Example:

- Yokohama (container)
  - Cup Noodles Museum exhibition galleries (E48)
  - My CUPNOODLES Factory (E49)
  - waterfront/date area (E57)
- Terrace House (container)
  - living room (H02)
  - dining area (H03)
  - kitchen (H04)
  - pool (H15)
- SHIBUYA 109 (container)
  - the specific interview/shop sales floor (E37); do not use “SHIBUYA 109” itself as the final atomic node once the tenant is identified.

## 5. Reliability warning

This is a high-coverage research inventory, not a certified shooting log. Public episode guides reliably establish the major outings, while contemporary Japanese location blogs identify a number of early restaurants. Later episodes have many unnamed restaurants, workrooms, shops, and event venues. Those entries are deliberately labeled generically instead of guessing. A truly exhaustive production-grade list would require a frame-by-frame pass through all 46 episodes, recording every on-screen location title card and splitting multi-room businesses wherever the camera enters distinct rooms.
