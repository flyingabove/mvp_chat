
# DEMO CASE: “THE QUIET AGREEMENT”
## (No Collusion, Detective Wins)

---

## Shared Backstory (Common Knowledge Before Interrogation)

Everyone knows these facts walking in:

- IU is dead (stab wound).
- Scene: Steve’s apartment, kitchen area.
- No forced entry.
- Time of death estimated: 11:40–12:15.
- Steve dated IU. Bob is Steve’s close friend.
- IU had been “worried about something” lately (friends said so).
- Police recovered no murder weapon.
- The apartment is tidy — almost too tidy.

---

## Hidden Truth (What Really Happened)

- IU confronted Steve about something financial (she found proof).
- Steve panicked, argued, then stabbed IU.
- Bob arrived during the argument, tried to calm it down, and later helped cover up:
  - wiped surfaces  
  - took the knife  
  - took IU’s phone  
  - deleted a message thread on Steve’s phone
- Bob also has his own exposure:
  - earlier that night he pressed IU hard to “drop it”
  - grabbed her wrist (leaving a bruise)
- Bob did not kill IU, but he is deeply implicated.
- They agreed in advance:
  - deny Bob was ever there
  - deny IU came over late
  - deny everything

---

## Legal Incentive (The Prisoner’s Dilemma)

Detective policy (commonly known to exist):

- First full cooperator may receive a deal / reduced sentence.
- Second cooperator receives much worse terms.
- If neither confesses, evidence may be insufficient to convict either beyond reasonable doubt.

Both suspects are rational, self-interested, and risk-calculating.

---

# ROUND 1 — Establish the “Safe Story” (Separate Rooms)

## Room A — Steve (First Interview)

**Detective:** Walk me through your night. Start at 10.  
**Steve:** Stayed in. Watched TV. IU didn’t come over.

**Detective:** Last time you saw her?  
**Steve:** Yesterday. We’re… not great right now.

**Detective:** Bob here tonight?  
**Steve:** No. Haven’t seen Bob today.

**Detective:** Any reason IU would show up late?  
**Steve:** No. She had her own place. She wasn’t here.

**Steve’s self-interest:**  
- keep it simple  
- deny IU’s presence  
- deny Bob  
- avoid timeline

---

## Room B — Bob (First Interview)

**Detective:** Walk me through your night.  
**Bob:** Drove around. Cleared my head. I didn’t see IU.

**Detective:** Were you at Steve’s?  
**Bob:** No.

**Detective:** You talk to Steve today?  
**Bob:** Texted. Nothing serious.

**Detective:** You two ever argue with IU?  
**Bob:** No. That was Steve’s relationship.

**Bob’s self-interest:**  
- distance from scene  
- distance from victim  
- frame as “not my relationship”

**Result after Round 1:**  
Both stick to “I wasn’t there / she wasn’t there.”

---

# ROUND 2 — Bend the Payoff Matrix (Still Separate)

## Back to Steve (Second Interview)

Detective has only soft, plausible facts:

- Neighbor heard a man’s voice and a woman crying (~11:50).
- Hallway camera shows a male silhouette entering (~11:45).
- Partial shoeprint in kitchen (not identifiable).
- IU’s phone last pinged near Steve’s building (coarse cell data).

**Detective:** IU’s phone was active near your building late. You sure she didn’t come by?  
**Steve:** Phones ping towers. Doesn’t mean anything.

**Detective:** Neighbor heard arguing around 11:50. A woman crying.  
**Steve:** Thin walls. Could be anyone.

**Detective:** Did Bob come over?  
**Steve:** No.

**Detective (risk statement):**  
Here’s what happens in cases like this: one person tells the whole truth first, they get a deal. Second guy doesn’t. I’ve seen it play out.

**Steve:** I’m not playing games.

Steve is still holding — barely.

---

## Back to Bob (Second Interview)

**Detective:** Bob, “I wasn’t there” is a common opening line. That’s fine.  
But understand this: first person who cooperates gets the best outcome.

**Bob:** I don’t know what you want me to say.

**Detective:** I want the truth before Steve decides you’re the one to carry it.

**Bob:** Steve’s emotional. He gets… intense.

**Detective:** Were you with him tonight?  
**Bob:** No.

**Result after Round 2:**  
Both still deny presence, but both now fear betrayal.

---

# ROUND 3 — Controlled Asymmetry (Crucial Round)

## Steve (Third Interview)

**Detective:** Bob’s car was near your building at 11:46.  
**Steve:** He drives everywhere.

**Detective:** He didn’t deny being near. He denied being inside.  
**Steve:** …So?

**Detective:** You’re both telling stories that protect you. That’s normal.  
But only one of you gets to be first.

**Detective (strategic wedge):**  
Bob is positioning himself. He’s saying you were worked up, and he’s asking what cooperation looks like.

Steve’s internal math:
- If Bob flips first, Steve is finished.
- Steve must control the narrative.

**Steve:** Fine. Bob came by for a minute.  
**Detective:** Inside?  
**Steve:** Yeah. Brief. But I left before things got bad.  
**Detective:** You left your own apartment?  
**Steve:** I stepped out. Air. I didn’t kill her.

Steve’s move:
- admits Bob was there  
- claims he left  
- avoids confession  
- sets Bob up as last-with-victim

---

## Bob (Third Interview)

**Detective:** Steve admits you were there.  
**Bob:** …What?

**Detective:** He says you came inside. And he says he left before things got bad.

Bob’s internal math:
- Steve is trying to pin the murder on Bob.
- Bob cannot be second.

**Bob:** I was there, yes. But I didn’t do anything.  
I walked in and felt something was off. Steve and IU were arguing.  
Steve was blocking the kitchen.

**Detective:** Did Steve leave?  
**Bob:** No. That’s a lie. He didn’t leave. He was right there.

**Result after Round 3:**  
Direct contradiction. No way to realign.

---

# ROUND 4 — The Deal Race

## Steve (Fourth Interview)

**Detective:** Bob admits he was inside.  
**Steve:** Of course he does.

**Detective:** He says you never left. He says you were blocking the kitchen.  
**Steve:** He’s lying.

**Detective:** He also says he’s willing to cooperate.

Steve believes the clock is ticking.

**Steve:** She came at me with accusations. She had papers.  
She was screaming. I grabbed her arm to calm her down.  
It got out of control.

**Detective:** Did you stab IU?  
**Steve:** …I didn’t mean to. It was a moment.

**Detective:** Where’s the knife?  
**Steve:** Bob took it. He said he’d “fix it.”

Steve confesses and implicates Bob.

---

## Bob (Fourth Interview)

**Detective:** Steve confessed. He says you took the knife.  
**Bob:** He— …

**Detective:** He’s cooperating first. That’s what it looks like.

Bob’s calculation:
- minimize murder charge
- accept accessory role

**Bob:** I didn’t stab her.  
But yes — after it happened, Steve panicked.  
I wiped things. I took the knife. I threw it off a bridge.

**Detective:** Why help him?  
**Bob:** Because if I didn’t, he’d say I did it.  
Exactly like he tried just now.

---

## Outcome — Detective Wins

Even without a weapon or DNA slam dunk:

- Steve confessed to stabbing.
- Bob admitted cover-up and disposal.
- Lies demonstrate consciousness of guilt.
- Motive emerges (papers, accusations).
- Timeline collapses their stories.

This only worked because they could not re-align.

---

## Why This Is a Strong Demo Case

- Second-order reasoning drives every move.
- Lies are payoff-driven, not random.
- Minimal hard evidence.
- Progress comes from:
  - contradictions  
  - belief manipulation  
  - controlled disclosure  
  - asymmetric information flow
